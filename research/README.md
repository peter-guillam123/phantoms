# Research notes

What the personas rest on, how well it has been checked, and what is
missing.

- [The market and the beliefs](market-and-beliefs.md): the magazines, what
  they paid, how an unknown got printed, and what people then thought a
  ghost was.
- Local history sits with each writer, in the "What is real" list at the
  end of each file in `personas/`.

## How it was done, and how far to trust it

Four research passes ran in parallel on 11 November 2026. This cloud
session's network settings at first blocked Wikipedia and most reference
sites, so those passes could search but could not open pages. They also
ran out of searches before they finished. Chris then allowed Wikipedia,
the Internet Archive and two magazine indexes, and I read about 50
Wikipedia articles directly to check the facts the personas lean on
hardest.

So every fact carries one of three marks:

- **(read)** I read the page text myself.
- **(extract)** It appeared in a search summary of the page named. Good,
  but one step short of reading it.
- **(memory)** I believe it, and found no page. Check before a story
  depends on it.

## What is solid

- The central event behind each writer: the Sheffield flood, Road Hill
  House, Maiwand, Coventina's Well, Bill o' Jack's and Gorse Hall,
  Bridget Cleary, the Davies ghost trial, Sarah Jacob, Thompson and
  Bywaters, Wheal Owles.

- The outline of what the psychical researchers, the spiritualists and

## What is thin

These are the gaps, roughly in order of how much they matter.

1. **The British Library book itself.** I have the contents list of Mike
   Ashley's "Glimpses of the Unknown" (2018) but not his notes on who the
   forgotten authors were, and not which magazine each story first ran
   in. Those notes are the best evidence there is for what an obscure
   ghost-story writer's life looked like. If the book that set you off is
   this one, or another in the series, photographs of the editor's
   introduction and author notes would help more than anything else.
2. **Money.** I have Arnold Bennett's 1903 figures for unknown writers
   (roughly half a guinea to three guineas per thousand words) and what
   the Strand paid Conan Doyle. I have nothing on what Blackwood's,
   Chambers's, the Irish Monthly, the Cotton Factory Times or the 1920s
   magazines paid. Every sum of money in a persona file is an estimate
   and says so.
3. **Working lives.** Not found: a private soldier's pay and a
   quartermaster's pension; a woman lecturer's salary; a village
   schoolmistress's salary and the marriage bar in Wales; typists' wages;
   a priest vicar's income at Wells; the age of entry and the syllabus at
   the Porthcurno cable school.
4. **The cable stations.** The best setting of the ten and the worst
   documented here. I want first-hand accounts of station life on
   Ascension and Cocos, and to know whether the staff magazine, The
   Zodiac, printed fiction. The PK Porthcurno museum's archive and the
   site atlantic-cable.com are the places to look. The second is still
   blocked in this session.
5. **Dialect and speech.** Kenworthy's Lancashire, Tobin's Tipperary
   English, Protheroe's Welsh English and Dalgleish's Scots are written
   from my general knowledge of period texts. Before the stories are
   written, each wants a real sample to copy from: a page of Ben Brierley
   or Allen Clarke, a Rosa Mulholland story, an Allen Raine chapter, a
   Blackwood's legal sketch.
6. **Folklore specific to the district.** Boggart tales from Saddleworth
   itself; Frome and Mendip traditions; the Irish belief that a soul does
   its purgatory at the place of its fault (found for Mayo, not for
   Tipperary).
7. **Small conflicts.** Where Constance Kent was tried (Salisbury or
   Devizes); the name and start date of the Royal Berkshire Regiment's
   journal; the end date of "Y Frythones"; the exact date Charles Peace
   jumped from the train.
8. **Names.** One quick search for real authors with the same names as
   the ten. Nothing found, but that is not proof.
