# George Ormiston (1862–1934)

*Invented.* Assistant editor of the real *Chambers's Journal*, Edinburgh.
He asked each of the ten for a story at some point. He is the only thing
that connects them.

The reader-facing version of this is `content/editor.md`.

## Why Chambers's

Chris asked for an invented editor on a real magazine, in a job obscure
enough to have left no trace. Chambers's fits better than any other
magazine I looked at:

- It ran from 1832 to 1956, so one man could have worked there across
  the whole span of the ten, from Tugwell (stopped 1905) to Gadd (started
  1921).
- It was cheap, respectable and read everywhere, and it took work from
  unknowns in the provinces. A Lancashire spinner and a Welsh
  schoolmistress are believable in it. They would not be in the Cornhill.
- Its named editor was a member of the owning family, who also ran the
  firm. The daily reading of manuscripts fell to staff whose names were
  never printed.
- Three of the ten (Lovegrove, Dalgleish, Trembath) were already down as
  selling to it.

## Life

- Born 1862 in the Canongate, Edinburgh, son of a compositor in the
  firm's printing office.
- Joined W. & R. Chambers at 339 High Street in 1877, aged 15, as a
  reader's boy. Moved to reading manuscripts in 1884. Assistant editor
  from 1896, a title used in the house and never printed. Retired 1929.
  Died 1934 at Portobello.
- Married Agnes; no children. Lunched for 30 years with Andrew
  Dalgleish, whose letters are the in-world source for his opinions.
- Published nothing. Signed margins "G. O."

## His taste

Dalgleish's summary: "George will take any ghost that has cost the teller
something." He sent back any story whose apparition could be swapped for
another without loss. He wanted the ghost to be the writer's own, got
from a place or a trade or a death.

This is also the editorial rule for the project. A story that could be
moved from one of the ten to another is not finished.

## What is real

Marks: (read) I read the page; (extract) seen in a search summary only;
(memory) unchecked.

- Chambers's Edinburgh Journal: first number 4 February 1832, a penny
  weekly; renamed Chambers's Journal at the end of 1897; ended 1956.
  (read)
  [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambers%27s_Edinburgh_Journal)
- The firm's building at 339 High Street, Edinburgh, sold to the city in
  the 1930s. (extract)
- Robert Chambers junior edited the Journal until he died in 1888. His
  son Charles E. S. Chambers (1859–1936) became editor and chairman of
  the firm. (extract) The exact year he took over is not confirmed.
- In January 1884 the Journal reported 3,225 manuscripts received in the
  past year and 330 accepted. (extract)
  [Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/64994) has issues
  of that series; I have not found the page itself.
- Conan Doyle's first story, "The Mystery of Sasassa Valley", ran
  unsigned on 6 September 1879 and earned him three guineas. (extract)
  [Arthur Conan Doyle Encyclopedia](https://www.arthur-conan-doyle.com/wiki/The_Mystery_of_Sasassa_Valley)
- The firm's papers are in the National Library of Scotland (Dep. 341),
  and include publication ledgers and literary correspondence. (extract)
  Worth knowing if the project grows a story of its own: a real archive
  exists in which an Ormiston could plausibly be looked for. The site
  does not claim he is in it.
- Not found: who actually read manuscripts at Chambers's in these years;
  the job titles the firm used; what it paid for a short story after
  1880; whether it reprinted from regimental journals. "Reader's boy" as
  a printing-office job is from memory.
